# Phase 0 literature memo — the two load-bearing questions (Sept 18, 2026)

All citations verified against ≥1 independent bibliographic source this session.

## Q1. Does subsoil POM/light-fraction enrichment after partial harvest have precedent?

**Verdict: the observation appears novel in its specifics; the mechanism is anticipated. Frame as: a documented but rarely quantified phenomenon, observed here at unusual magnitude (2.6×) and depth (20–40 cm), with unresolved fate.**

- **Nave, L.E., Vance, E.D., Swanston, C.W., Curtis, P.S. (2010).** Harvest impacts on soil carbon storage in temperate forests. *For. Ecol. Manage.* 259:857–866. Meta-analysis (432 response ratios): harvest reduces forest-floor C (~30% hardwoods), mineral-soil effects minimal — but explicitly notes mineral-soil *increases* can occur via **incorporation of harvest residues into mineral soil**. Our direct precedent for the burial mechanism.
- **Diochon, A.C., Kellman, L. (2009).** Physical fractionation of SOM: destabilization of deep soil carbon following harvesting. *J. Geophys. Res. Biogeosci.* 114, G01016, doi:10.1029/2008JG000844. Red spruce chronosequence: deep (>20 cm) light-fraction/mineral C *declines* over decades post-harvest — the decay trajectory our 2-yr enrichment may be the front end of. Companion: **Diochon, Kellman & Beltrami (2009)** *For. Ecol. Manage.* 257:413–420 (SOC to 50 cm depleted ~50% within 30 yr post-clearcut).
- **Mayer, M. et al. (2020).** Influence of forest management on soil organic carbon stocks: a knowledge synthesis. *For. Ecol. Manage.* 466:118127. The field's synthesis; documents organic-horizon losses, small mineral-soil effects, site-factor dependence. Cite for the consensus backdrop our result cuts across.
- Older USFS literature (e.g., Cromack et al. 1979, cited in GTR INT-280) describes post-harvest surface-mineral SOM increases from forest-floor/slash mixing as **"usually short-lived"** — supports the pulse-vs-persistence question as the open one.
- Deep-burial literature (e.g., Alcántara et al. 2017, deep-ploughing, *Sci. Rep.*): OM buried in subsoil can persist and even stabilize — the counterweight: burial does not guarantee rapid loss.

**Implication for the paper:** we cannot claim "novel mechanism," we CAN claim first quantification of subsoil light-fraction enrichment under diameter-limit cutting in Appalachian hardwoods, with a REF/STS contrast, censoring-robust statistics, and an explicit fate question (pulse → Diochon-style decline vs burial-stabilization). The treatment-vs-time-since-harvest confound (2 vs 9 yr) must be stated; the Diochon chronosequence actually helps interpret both readings.

## Q2. Do mature temperate hardwood stands keep accumulating carbon?

**Verdict: our reference watershed's continued accumulation (1.34 Mg C/ha/yr at ~110 yr, no saturation through 2001, extrapolation matching 2022 stocks) is consistent with, and a clean single-site demonstration of, established literature. Frame as confirmatory support, not a novel claim — which is the safer and equally useful position.**

- **Luyssaert, S. et al. (2008).** Old-growth forests as global carbon sinks. *Nature* 455:213–215. NEP usually positive in forests 15–800 yr old.
- **Stephenson, N.L. et al. (2014).** Rate of tree carbon accumulation increases continuously with tree size. *Nature* 507:90–93. 403 species: large old trees fix disproportionate carbon — mechanistically matches our finding that the REF difference sits almost entirely in trees ≥50 cm.
- Supporting if needed: Zhou et al. (2006) *Science* 314:1417 (old-growth soils accumulate C); regional aging-forest NEP work (Curtis & Gough) can be added at manuscript stage.
